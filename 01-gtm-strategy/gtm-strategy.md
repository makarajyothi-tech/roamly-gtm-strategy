# GTM Strategy · Roamly Groups

## 1. Discover Framework

### Align on the Goal
- **Core business problem:** Roamly is losing potential group bookings because its platform doesn't support coordinated bookings, shared payments, and itinerary planning, resulting in customer frustration and high drop-off rates.
- **What success looks like:** Success means Roamly becomes the preferred platform for both individual and group experiences, offering seamless coordination and flexible payments. It strengthens customer trust, encourages repeat bookings, and drives organic growth as travelers bring their friends onto the platform.

### Do the Research
- **Signals of a real opportunity:** Existing customers are already attempting group bookings through separate accounts, manually coordinating payments and availability. High drop-off rates and repeated support feedback indicate unmet demand. Customers are trying to use a feature that doesn't exist, suggesting a strong opportunity to improve conversion and unlock group bookings.
- **Top 5 possible segments:** 1.Existing Roamly customers traveling with friends
2.Families traveling together
3.Groups of independent travelers
4.Celebration and occasion groups
5.Corporate teams
- **How to size the opportunity:** To size the opportunity, I'd analyze how many customers attempt group bookings, their drop-off rates, average group size, booking value and potential conversion uplift. I'd use product analytics, booking data, customer support tickets and user interviews. I'd also research competitors and group travel trends to understand the broader market potential

### Build a Working Team
- **Cross-functional stakeholders:** I'd involve Product, Engineering, UX, Data Analytics, Marketing, Customer Support, Host Partnerships, Finance and Legal. Together, we'd validate customer demand, assess operational and technical feasibility, develop the positioning, and ensure a smooth and commercially viable launch

### Form Your Hypothesis
- **Most valuable segment to target first, and why:** Existing Roamly customers who travel with friends would be my first target segment. They already trust the platform and have demonstrated booking intent. By removing group coordination and payment friction, we can improve conversion, encourage repeat bookings and acquire new customers through group invitations.

You are prioritizing a segment with:

1.Existing brand familiarity
2. Demonstrated demand
3. Lower potential customer acquisition costs
4. Organic referral opportunities
5. Strong potential for repeat usage
- **Core value proposition:** Roamly Groups lets friends discover, plan and book local experiences together, with shared itineraries, coordinated availability and split payments, all in one seamless experience

## 4. The assumption I'm pushing back on

Your biggest blind spot: Assuming existing customers will generate expansion

You've chosen Expand as your primary goal.

That's reasonable, but you're assuming that enabling group bookings will increase total business rather than simply change how existing bookings happen.

Consider two scenarios.

Scenario A:
Four friends previously booked individually. Now they book together through Roamly Groups.

The booking experience improves, but Roamly may generate no additional revenue.

Scenario B:
One existing customer invites three friends who wouldn't otherwise have booked on Roamly.

Now you've created incremental demand.

Those are fundamentally different commercial outcomes.

My challenge:

"How will you distinguish incremental bookings from bookings that would have happened anyway?"

I'd want to measure incremental completed bookings and contribution margin, not just group booking volume.

There's also a second assumption worth testing: that hosts can reliably accommodate the group sizes and shared availability your product promises.

If customers organize and pay successfully but hosts cannot fulfill the experience, you've created a bigger trust problem.

## 5. V1 GTM strategy one-pager

### Roamly Groups | GTM Strategy V1

**Launch approach:** Validate → Pilot → Scale
**Primary goal:** Expansion
**GTM motion:** Product Led Growth (PLG), supported by Community Led Growth (CLG)

##### 1. Business Opportunity

Roamly customers are already attempting group bookings using separate accounts, manually coordinating availability and payments. High abandonment and repeated support feedback indicate unmet demand.

**Opportunity:** Remove booking friction to unlock incremental group bookings, increase customer value and introduce new travelers to Roamly.

**Key assumption to validate:** Group bookings will generate additional demand rather than simply replace individual bookings.

##### 2. Target Segment

**Primary:** Existing Roamly customers who have demonstrated group-booking intent, particularly those traveling with friends.

**Why this segment?** Existing brand trust, demonstrated demand, potentially lower acquisition costs and opportunities for organic referrals.

**Secondary:** New travelers invited by existing customers.

##### 3. Value Proposition

**"Explore together. Book together. Pay your way."**

Roamly Groups enables friends to coordinate experiences, manage shared itineraries and split payments without juggling multiple reservations or collecting money manually.

##### 4. Recommended Channel Approach

| Channel                            | Strategy                                                                         | Objective                                |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **In-product discovery (Primary)** | Introduce "Book with Friends" on experience pages and during checkout.           | Convert existing booking intent.         |
| **CRM: Email & Push**              | Target customers showing group-booking behavior or previous booking abandonment. | Reactivate high-intent users.            |
| **Group Invitations**              | Enable customers to invite friends through WhatsApp or shareable links.          | Drive organic acquisition.               |
| **Travel Communities (Secondary)** | Partner with selected travel communities and relevant creators.                  | Validate community-driven demand.        |
| **Host Network**                   | Pilot with hosts capable of accommodating group experiences.                     | Ensure supply readiness and fulfillment. |

**Recommendation:** Prioritize owned channels before investing in paid acquisition.

##### 5. Launch Strategy

**Phase 1: Validate**

* Analyze group-booking abandonment and customer intent.
* Interview customers who attempted group bookings.
* Identify hosts and cities with sufficient group capacity.
* Establish baseline conversion, booking value and contribution margin.

**Phase 2: Pilot**

* Launch in 2–3 cities with verified host availability.
* Target existing customers demonstrating group-booking intent.
* Test contextual in-product prompts and group invitations.
* Compare outcomes against a randomized control group.

**Phase 3: Scale**

* Expand to additional cities only after demonstrating incremental commercial value.
* Activate travel communities and referral-led acquisition.
* Optimize onboarding, payments and group coordination based on pilot learnings.

##### 6. Success Metrics

**Primary success metric:**

**Incremental completed bookings per eligible customer**, measured as the difference between pilot and control groups.

This distinguishes genuine expansion from existing individual bookings simply shifting to group checkout.

| Supporting metric                      | What it validates             |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Group booking conversion rate          | Reduction in booking friction |
| Invitation-to-booking conversion       | New customer activation       |
| Incremental contribution margin        | Commercial sustainability     |
| Host fulfillment rate                  | Operational reliability       |
| Payment failure and cancellation rates | Booking experience quality    |

**Suggested pilot decision:** Scale only if the pilot demonstrates positive incremental contribution margin, measurable booking uplift and acceptable operational performance. Set numerical thresholds before launch.

##### 7. Strategic Hypothesis

**IF** Roamly enables customers with demonstrated group-booking intent to coordinate, book and split payments seamlessly,

**THEN** incremental completed bookings and customer value will increase,

**BECAUSE** removing coordination and payment friction will convert previously abandoned group bookings and encourage customers to invite additional travelers.

**Measured through:** Incremental completed bookings, contribution margin and invitation-driven new customer conversions.

---

##### V1 Executive Recommendation

Launch Roamly Groups through a **PLG-first, controlled rollout targeting existing high-intent customers**, supported by selective community activation.

Prioritize proving three things before scaling:

1. **Demand:** Customers complete bookings they otherwise would have abandoned.
2. **Commercial value:** Group bookings generate incremental, profitable business.
3. **Operational readiness:** Hosts can reliably fulfill group experiences.

**The strategic shift:** We're not launching Roamly Groups simply because customers want split payments. We're testing whether solving group-booking friction creates a scalable, profitable growth opportunity.

**V1 status:** Working hypothesis. Segment size, launch economics, channel effectiveness and success thresholds require validation before full GTM investment.
